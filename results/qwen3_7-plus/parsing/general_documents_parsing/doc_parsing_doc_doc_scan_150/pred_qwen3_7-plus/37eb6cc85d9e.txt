\textbf{Mean} & $\bar{x}$ = sample mean \\
$$ \bar{x} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i $$ & $n$ = size of the sample \\
\textbf{Standard Deviation*} & $s$ = sample standard deviation (i.e., the sample-based estimate of the standard deviation of the population) \\
$$ S = \sqrt{\frac{\sum(x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n-1}} $$ & \\
\textbf{Standard Error of the Mean} & $o$ = observed results \\
$$ SE_{\bar{x}} = \frac{S}{\sqrt{n}} $$ & $e$ = expected results \\
\textbf{Chi-Square} & Degrees of freedom are equal to the number of distinct possible outcomes minus one. \\
$$ \chi^2 = \sum \frac{(o-e)^2}{e} $$ & \\
