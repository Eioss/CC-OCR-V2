Based on the information provided in the infographic, the **fatality rate** is calculated using a specific formula from the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS).

The footnote at the bottom of the image states:

> *“The fatality rate is per 100,000 full-time workers or equivalent, based on the Bureau of Labor Statistics’ formula.”*

It further clarifies the formula:

> *“The formula is the number of fatal injuries divided by number of hours worked by all employees during the calendar year, then multiplied by 200,000,000.”*

---

### Breakdown of the Formula:

1.  **Numerator**: Number of fatal work injuries in a given occupation/industry during the year.
2.  **Denominator**: Total number of hours worked by *all* employees in that occupation/industry during the same year.
3.  **Multiplier**: 200,000,000

This multiplier represents the total hours worked by 100,000 full-time equivalent workers in a year:
- 100,000 workers × 40 hours/week × 50 weeks/year = **200,000,000 hours**

So, the formula essentially calculates how many fatalities would occur if exactly 100,000 people worked full-time for a year in that job.

---

### In Simple Terms:

> **Fatality Rate = (Number of Fatalities / Total Hours Worked) × 200,000,000**

This standardizes the rate across different occupations and industries, allowing for fair comparison regardless of the actual size of the workforce.

---

✅ **Answer Summary:**
The fatality rate is calculated as:  
**(Number of fatal injuries ÷ Total hours worked by all employees in the year) × 200,000,000**,  
which gives the number of fatalities per 100,000 full-time equivalent workers.
