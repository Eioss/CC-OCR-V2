### 🗳️ **The System: How Do the Candidates Win?**

The process happens in stages:

1.  **Caucuses and State Primaries**  
    → Voters in each state (and territory) participate in caucuses or primary elections to express their preference for a presidential nominee within their party.

2.  **Party Delegates Are Chosen**  
    → Based on the results of these primaries/caucuses, each state sends **delegates** to its respective **National Party Convention**. The number of delegates is often proportional to the vote share (though some states use winner-take-all).

3.  **National Party Conventions**  
    → At these conventions (one for Democrats, one for Republicans), the **party delegates officially choose the party’s candidate for president**. This is typically a formality if one candidate has secured a majority of delegates during the primaries, but it can be contested if no candidate reaches the threshold beforehand.

> ✅ So, while voters *influence* the choice through primaries/caucuses, the **official selection** of the presidential candidate happens at the **National Party Conventions**, by the **delegates** elected from each state.

---

### 🎯 Final Goal: Win the General Election

Once both parties have chosen their candidates, they compete in the **general election**, where the winner is determined not by popular vote alone, but by securing at least **270 Electoral College votes**.
