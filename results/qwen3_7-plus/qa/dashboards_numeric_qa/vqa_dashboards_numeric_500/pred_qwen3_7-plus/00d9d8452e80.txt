The area between the wickets is called the **pitch**.

In cricket, the pitch is the rectangular strip of ground (typically 22 yards or 20.12 meters long) located in the center of the field, where most of the action takes place — bowlers deliver the ball toward the batsman standing at the opposite end, and the two sets of wickets are placed at each end of this strip.

As shown in the infographic, the pitch is the central playing surface that requires careful maintenance — including moisture control (ideally starting a Test match at 32% moisture) and effective drainage (like Trent Bridge’s system) to cope with rain and ensure play can continue safely and fairly.

So, while “the crease” refers to the lines marking the batter’s or bowler’s position, and “the outfield” is the grassy area beyond the pitch, the specific term for the area *between* the wickets is:

> **The Pitch**

*(Sometimes also referred to as “the middle” or “the strip,” but officially and technically: the pitch.)*
