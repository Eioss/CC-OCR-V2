The first feature-length film released with synchronized sound was **The Jazz Singer (1927)**.

As shown in the infographic, it is credited as the “First feature-length film with synchronized sound,” marking a revolutionary moment in cinema history by integrating spoken dialogue and musical numbers directly into the film’s narrative — breaking from the silent film era.

While earlier experiments with sound existed (like short films or partial sound sequences), *The Jazz Singer* is widely recognized as the breakthrough that launched the “talkie” era in Hollywood.

---

**Note**: The film wasn’t entirely “all-talking” — most of it still used intertitles and live orchestral accompaniment, but its synchronized singing and speaking scenes were groundbreaking enough to change the industry forever.
