Yugoslavia pardoned **Laszlo Toth**, a Thornton, Colorado resident who had been jailed for industrial espionage. He was arrested in Yugoslavia in August 1975 after allegedly taking photographs of a beet-sugar plant. The pardon was announced on July 15, 1976, though his exact release date was not yet known at the time of the article (July 16, 1976).

The pardon was granted by the Yugoslav collective presidency, which included President Tito. His wife, Zora Toth, expressed delight but noted she would only feel fully relieved once he was back in the United States.

U.S. officials, including Rep. Jim Johnson’s office and the U.S. Embassy in Belgrade, had been working to secure his release. The embassy confirmed the pardon as “the first solid thing we had,” though they were still awaiting a specific release date.

Toth, a naturalized American citizen and employee of the Great Western Sugar Co., had been sentenced to seven years in prison after a closed-door trial. Rumors of his impending release had circulated for months, but this was the first official confirmation.
