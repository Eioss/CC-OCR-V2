This table, titled **“Class Performance Recorded After Taking the Course on Food and Nutrition”**, presents data comparing how two classes — the **1975 Class** and the **1976 Class** — performed on a 50-question test after completing a course on food and nutrition.

The performance is measured as the **percentage of students agreeing with the course teachings** across different categories of questions:

1. **17 questions relating to established economic or scientific facts**  
   - 1975 Class: 88% agreement  
   - 1976 Class: 86% agreement

2. **17 questions relating to confirmed scientific findings but still rejected by consumer activists**  
   - 1975 Class: 82% agreement  
   - 1976 Class: 78% agreement

3. **16 questions relating to issues pitting professional scientists’ opinions against charges by consumer activists**  
   - 1975 Class: 83% agreement  
   - 1976 Class: 80% agreement

4. **Entire test (50 questions total)**  
   - 1975 Class: 84% overall agreement  
   - 1976 Class: 81% overall agreement

---

### Summary:
The table shows that **both classes generally agreed with the course teachings**, but the **1975 class consistently showed higher agreement percentages** than the 1976 class across all question categories and overall. The largest drop in agreement from 1975 to 1976 was in the category involving “confirmed scientific findings rejected by consumer activists” (from 82% to 78%).

It suggests that while the course was effective in conveying its teachings, there may have been a slight decline in student alignment with those teachings between the two years — possibly reflecting changing attitudes, external influences (like consumer activism), or differences in cohort composition.
