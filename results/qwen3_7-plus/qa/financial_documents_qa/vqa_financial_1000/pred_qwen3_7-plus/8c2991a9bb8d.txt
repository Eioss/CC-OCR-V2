This form is known as a **Universal Credit Card Charge Form**.

It was used by **Eastern Airlines** (as indicated by the logo and name on the form) to process credit card payments for airline tickets or related charges. The form includes fields for:

- Passenger information
- Routing details (e.g., “NYC/Newark” to “ASH DC”)
- Fare breakdown
- Credit card number and expiration date
- Airline, form, and serial numbers for tracking
- A statement that tickets are non-transferable and no cash refunds are allowed

The phrase “CONTRACTOR INVOICE COPY” suggests this particular copy was retained by Eastern Airlines (or its contractor) for accounting purposes — likely one of multiple copies generated during the transaction (Page 7 of 8, with Page 8 on reverse).

These forms were common in the pre-digital era (1960s–1980s) when airlines manually processed credit card transactions using carbon-copy paper forms before electronic point-of-sale systems became widespread.
