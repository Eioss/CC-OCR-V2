Based on the infographic provided, **infrared** touchscreens are more durable than resistive ones.

Here’s the breakdown from the “DURABILITY” row:

- **Resistive**:  
  > *Fair Durability. Screens are vulnerable to scratches and other minor damage. The screen will wear over time and need replacing.*

- **Infrared (Heat-Sensitive and Optical)**:  
  > *Good Durability. Glass shatters upon major impact only.*

### Why?
- Resistive screens use a flexible top layer (usually polyester film) that can scratch, wear out, or degrade with repeated pressure.
- Infrared screens typically use glass as the surface material — which is harder and more resistant to everyday wear and tear. They only fail under major physical impact (like dropping or crushing).

> ✅ So, **infrared > resistive** in terms of durability.

*(Note: Capacitive also has “Good Durability” like infrared — but since your question was specifically between resistive and infrared, infrared wins.)*

---

**Bonus insight**: While infrared is more durable, it’s also the most expensive to make ($$$), and its optical version requires bare fingers or a stylus — unlike resistive, which works with gloves or any object. So durability isn’t the only factor — usability and cost matter too!
