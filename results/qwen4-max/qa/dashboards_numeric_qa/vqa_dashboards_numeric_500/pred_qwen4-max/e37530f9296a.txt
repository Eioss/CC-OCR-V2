The fatality rate is calculated using the Bureau of Labor Statistics’ formula, as stated in the footnote at the bottom of the infographic:

> **The fatality rate is per 100,000 full-time workers or equivalent**, based on the Bureau of Labor Statistics’ formula.  
> **The formula is: the number of fatal injuries divided by the number of hours worked by all employees during the calendar year, then multiplied by 200,000,000.**

---

### Why 200,000,000?

This multiplier standardizes the rate to represent **100,000 full-time equivalent (FTE) workers**.

- A full-time worker is assumed to work **2,000 hours per year** (40 hours/week × 50 weeks).
- So, 100,000 FTE workers × 2,000 hours = **200,000,000 hours**.

Thus, multiplying the ratio (fatalities ÷ total hours worked) by 200,000,000 gives you the number of fatalities **per 100,000 full-time workers** — making it easier to compare risk across occupations with different numbers of workers or hours.

---

✅ In short:  
**Fatality Rate = (Number of Fatalities / Total Hours Worked) × 200,000,000**  
→ This equals fatalities per 100,000 full-time workers.
