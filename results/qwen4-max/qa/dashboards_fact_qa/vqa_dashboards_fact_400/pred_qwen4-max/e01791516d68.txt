Based on the "Phases" section in the center of *The Map of Chemistry* infographic, the different phases of matter shown are:

1. **Solid** – depicted as a yellow cube
2. **Liquid** – depicted as a purple flowing shape
3. **Gas** – depicted as scattered white particles
4. **Plasma** – depicted as a pink/magenta energetic burst (labeled near "not binary or explosive")

These four are the classical/fundamental states of matter commonly taught in chemistry:

- **Solid**: particles tightly packed in a fixed structure; definite shape and volume.
- **Liquid**: particles close but able to flow; definite volume, takes the shape of its container.
- **Gas**: particles far apart and moving freely; no fixed shape or volume.
- **Plasma**: an ionized gas of charged particles; found in stars, lightning, and neon signs.

The map also hints at more exotic states (e.g., Bose–Einstein condensates) under the "not binary or explosive" note, but the four primary phases illustrated are **solid, liquid, gas, and plasma**.
