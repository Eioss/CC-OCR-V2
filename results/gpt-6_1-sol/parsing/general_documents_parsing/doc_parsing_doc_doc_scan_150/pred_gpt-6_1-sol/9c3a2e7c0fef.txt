{\small FRAGMENTS OF NORN}

\begin{center}
\textit{A proverbial Phrase.}
\end{center}

Dea dombvidla vōga dea vidla vōga pēsa (or \textit{passion}) vōga.
Given with following explanation: \textit{Easter-Sunday weather will last
all the week.} More correct probably: \textit{the weather of Passion-week
will be the weather of Easter-week.}

The Conningsburgh phrase (acc. to Low): ``Myrk in e Liora, Luc
in e Liunga, Tim in e Guest in e geungna'' (It's dark in the Chimney,
but it's light thro' the Heath, it's still time for the stranger to
be gone).

In Yell (in ``de Herra'') this phrase is still preserved in the following
form:

Mōrka lōra, lestra linga, tāmra gestra gǫngara [myrkt er í ljóra,
ljóst er í lyngi, tími er, at gestr(inn) gengr]. With regard to the form
gǫngara, cf. Fær. gongur.

\bigskip

Our Lord's Prayer\footnote{A man still living in Unst was formerly able,
according to his own and other's statements, to recite Our Lord's Prayer
in Norn, or at any rate, the greater part of it. Some time ago, however,
he had an illness which gradually enfeebled his memory, and when I looked
him up, he could only remember the following corrupted fragments;
\textit{halag warta name (hallowed be Thy name) --- --- sae himblins
(as in Heaven).}}
from Foula (reported by Low in his ``Tour thro' Orkney and Shetland'',
reprinted by Barry and in ``Annaler for nordisk oldkyndighed'' 1860).

Fy vor o er i Chimeri. Halaght vara nam det. La Konungdum
din cumma. La vill din vera gerurde i vrildin sinda eri Chimeri. Gav
vus dagh u dagloght brau. Forgive sindor wasa (wara?) sin vi forgiva
gøm a o sinda gainst wus. Lia wus eke o vera tempa, but delivra
wus fro adlu idlu, for do i ir Konungdum, u puri, u glori. Amen.

\begin{center}
An Incantation.
\end{center}

This is found in the tale: ``Da Tief i' de Neean'' (Shetland
Times 1879).

The tale itself, as well as the language used in it doubtless
originates from A.I. (Wests.).

The formula, which is mainly in Scoto-English, has Norn words
sprinkled throughout, some of which show old grammatical endings,
that have lost their significance. It runs as follows:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{l}
Da stuhl es scarp an fien,\\
Da sacheles emer a snean,\\
Da vird es siccr en puna,\\
A glimer F' mirk-as-dim hura,\\
La stuhl an vird ay gyrda\\
An prof er an skyla, an svirda.
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
