\section*{\textit{Formulas For a Line}}

You can think of the slope-intercept form of an equation for a
line, \(y = mx + b\), as a formula for the line given \(m\) and \(b\).

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{\textit{Example:}} Write the formula for a line given two points
\((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\).

\medskip

Use the idea that the slope of a line is the same
between any two points.
\[
\frac{y-y_1}{x-x_1}=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}
\]

\noindent
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\begin{tabular}{@{}l@{}}
Multiply each\\
side by \((x-x_1)\).
\end{tabular}
&
\(\displaystyle y-y_1=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}(x-x_1)\)
&
\begin{tabular}{@{}l@{}}
\textit{Two-point}\\
\textit{formula}
\end{tabular}
\end{tabular}

\medskip

If you know two points of a line, you can write an equation for
the line directly using this formula.

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{\textit{Use the two-point formula to write an equation for the line
that passes through the given points.}}
