\noindent The combined membership \(<\)15 years of age for SUAT and SEMI decreased from approximately 25 000 in 1997 to 6500 in 2005. The number of varicella cases treated as outpatients per 1000 varicella cases in the two member systems was 38.8 and overall 16.2, 64.8, 44.9 and 13.9 for the \(<\)1, 1--4, 5--9 and 10--14 years age groups, respectively. \noindent \textbf{DISCUSSION}

\noindent The results of this retrospective study demonstrate significant reductions in the number of varicella hospitalizations in a large public paediatric hospital (up to 86\% in the age group eligible for vaccination) compared to the pre-vaccination years 1997--1999. Furthermore, a substantial decrease was also observed in the ambulatory cases seen at two ambulatory medical systems (fig 2). Moreover, in the cases of varicella requiring medical attention, clinical varicella, when it occurred in vaccinated subjects, was associated with significantly fewer and less severe symptoms and complications than disease in unvaccinated children.

\noindent The observed decrease in varicella incidence is neither linear, nor constant. The initial sharp decline in 1999 is likely to be due to cyclic increase and decrease of varicella rather than the vaccination campaign. However, the persistence of the decrease after the vaccination campaign is strong evidence to assert that sustained reductions in case numbers and hospitalizations observed in the years following the onset of vaccination represent the true impact of the varicella vaccination programme. Without the varicella vaccination campaign, the number of varicella cases in the country would probably increase after 2000. This is supported by data from Buenos Aires, an adjacent province in Argentina which has no varicella mass vaccination, where a doubling of cases was reported from 2001 to 2002.
