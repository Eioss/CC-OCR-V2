We propose that 26 G>A polymorphism in the 5' UTR of \textit{BRCA2} has a regulatory role and acts as one of the risk factors in the initiation of breast cancer. This polymorphism may also influence the pathogenesis, further influenced by codon 72 polymorphism in the \textit{p53} gene.

The functional characterization of the common G>A polymorphism in the \textit{BRCA2} -26 5' UTR by reporter assay revealed that the A allele led to higher expression than the G allele in both HeLa and MCF-7 cell lines. This suggests that the polymorphism might have a role in the regulation of \textit{BRCA2} expression. Updating the same trend by ADR treatment further implied that the 26 polymorphism might regulate \textit{BRCA2} expression independent of other factors, specifically the expression of the \textit{BRCA2} promoter by ADR via p53 activation [21]. A search for a consensus sequence for the putative transcription factor binding site using the MatInspector program revealed a C/EBP alpha transcription binding site by the change from G>A in the -26 polymorphic position. However, the presence of an OCT1 binding site almost overlapping to this region, not affected by polymorphism, makes the transcriptional control hypothesis an unlikely one, unless proven by the gel shift or chromatin immunoprecipitation analysis. The analysis of the 5' UTR for RNA secondary structure by two different programs showed that the presence of G at 26 stabilized the loop at the vicinity of the translation start site, which could decrease the efficiency of the translation and thereby decrease the expression of \textit{BRCA2}. This finding of ours is supported by the classic work of Vega and colleagues on thrombopoietin mRNA translation, where the G allele showed a significant negative effect on eukaryotic mRNA translation \textit{in vivo} depending upon its stability and localization within the molecule.

The -26 G>A polymorphism in the 5' UTR of \textit{BRCA2} has been analyzed in some cancer types. In one study, the 26A allele was reported to show a borderline association with ovarian cancer, but similar results in breast cancer with \textit{BRCA1} mutation carriers were not found [49]. Another study showed a monotonic increase in the frequency of the heterozygous G/A genotype from the lowest to the highest risk groups of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma from northern China [50].
