\section*{The Discriminant}

The formula for finding the discriminant is derived
from the quadratic equation.

Discriminant formula: \(b^2 - 4ac\)

When:

\(\ast\ b^2 - 4ac > 0\)

\(\quad\ast\) there are two unequal real solutions.

\(\quad\ast\) if the discriminant is a perfect square,
the solutions are rational.

\(\quad\ast\) if the discriminant is not a perfect square,
the solutions are irrational

\(\ast\ b^2 - 4ac = 0\)

\(\quad\ast\) there is one solution that is a real number

\(\ast\ b^2 - 4ac < 0\)

\(\quad\ast\) there are no real solutions

\(\quad\ast\) there are two imaginary solutions

\(\quad\ast\) the solutions are complex conjugates
