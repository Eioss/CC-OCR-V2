\noindent\underline{Introduction.}\\
The language FEA was developed to facilitate semantic information retrieval in litera-\\
ture about abstract algebra. FEA has since proved useful as a means of stating program\\
assertions, and may be used also as a non-procedural programming language.

\medskip
\noindent The paper is divided into four sections:\\
\hspace*{2em}1. An informal introduction to FEA\\
\hspace*{2em}2. The possible use of FEA in information retrieval\\
\hspace*{2em}3. The possible use of FEA in program validation\\
\hspace*{2em}4. The formal definition of FEA.

\medskip
\noindent 1. An informal introduction to FEA.\\
In this section the language FEA - Formal English for Algebra (or Assertions) is described\\
informally. References to the formal definition in section 4 is given in the form\\
(F20 - F22) which refers to the productions numbered F20, F21, F22.

\medskip
\noindent FEA is a programming language. It is a formal, unambiguous language that:\\
1. Can be understood by mathematically mature persons.\\
2. Can be used to paraphrase theorems, hypotheses, rules of inference and\\
\hspace*{2em}(algebraic) facts. These paraphrases are called stellings. (The word stellings\\
\hspace*{2em}is coined to ensure that no confusion with the term ``proposition'', which is used in\\
\hspace*{2em}logic, can arise).\\
3. Can, in certain cases, be used to construct algorithms automatically.

\medskip
\noindent The syntax and most of the semantics of FEA is defined in terms of PLISP. PLISP is a\\
high level language for LISP, and is the subject of a paper in preparation. The PLISP\\
that is used in this paper is effectively self-explanatory, but notes will be added\\
wherever confusion may arise.

\medskip
\noindent\underline{Elementary Atoms (F42 - F45)}: An atom is a predicate applied to one or more terms.\\
A term is a variable, or is a function of other terms. The syntax of a term\\
will depend on the system in which FEA is embedded. In this paper a term is any\\
LISP expression.\\
E.g. The following are \texttt{<atom>}'s:\\
\hspace*{3em}\(X\ \mathrm{EQUALS}\ Y+1\)\\
\hspace*{3em}\(B\in S2\cup S3\)\\
\hspace*{3em}\(T3\subset T25\cap T15\)\\
\hspace*{3em}\(10>N+J-6\)

\medskip
\noindent In general the notation of formal logic is allowed in FEA. This means that \texttt{<atom>}'s\\
can be written in the form: \texttt{<predicate>}\quad \texttt{(<termlist>)}\\
E.g. \(\mathrm{REAL}(X)\)\\
\hspace*{3em}\(\mathrm{BETWEEN}\ (X; K; W)\)\\
\hspace*{3em}\(\mathrm{IS\text{-}RING}\ (T; S; O+; Ox)\)\\
\hspace*{3em}\(\mathrm{SUBSET}\ (B\cap S; U B)\)

\medskip
\noindent Other forms of \texttt{<atom>}'s that correspond closer to a natural language are also allowed\\
in FEA.

\medskip
\noindent\underline{Elementary Quantified Formulae (F1 - F3)}: A quantified well formed formula is a well\\
formed formula with zero or one or more quantifiers preceding it.

\medskip
\noindent A quantified well formed formula, or \texttt{<qwff>}, is the basic unit in a FEA stelling.
