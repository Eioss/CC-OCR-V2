A search for a consensus sequence for the putative transcription factor in the polymorphic site revealed the creation of a C/EBP alpha transcription binding site by the change from G\(>\)A in the -26 polymorphic region. However, the presence of an OCT1 binding site almost overlapping to this region, could affected by the polymorphism, makes the transcriptional control hypothesis an unlikely one, unless driven by the gel shift or chromatin immunoprecipitation. The analysis of the \(5'\) UTR for the RNA secondary structure by two different programs showed that the presence of G at -26 stabilized the loop at the vicinity of the translation start site, which could decrease the efficiency of the translation and thereby decrease the expression of \textit{BRCA2}. Results of ours is supported by the classic work of Vega Laso and colleagues [47] and Kozak [48], who have argued that the extent of hairpin negative effect on eukaryotic mRNA translation \textit{in vivo} depends upon its stability and localization within the molecule.

The -26 G\(>\)A polymorphism in the \(5'\) UTR of \textit{BRCA2} has been analyzed in some cancer types. In one study, the -26 A allele was found to show a `borderline association' with ovarian cancer, but similar results in breast cancer with \textit{BRCA1} mutation carriers were not found [49]. Another study showed a monotonic increase in the frequency of the heterozygous G/A genotype from the lowest to highest risk groups of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma from northern China [50].
