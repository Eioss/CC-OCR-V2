\section*{Formulas For a Line}

You can think of the slope-intercept form of an equation for a 
line, $y = mx + b$, as a formula for the line given $m$ and $b$.

\textbf{\textit{Example:}} Write the formula for a line given two points $(x_1, y_1)$ 
and $(x_2, x_2)$.

\quad Use the idea that the slope of a line is the same 
\quad between any two points.

\[
\frac{y - y_1}{x - x_1} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}
\]

Multiply each \qquad $y - y_1 = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}(x - x_1)$ \qquad \textit{Two-point} \\
side by $(x - x_1)$. \hfill \textit{formula}

If you know two points of a line, you can write an equation for 
the line directly using this formula.
