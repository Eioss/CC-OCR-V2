Many forms of spatial neglect have been described. Patients with neglect may ignore stimuli on one side of space, usually the left side. They may fail to dress or groom one side of the body, eat food from only one side of a plate, or bump into objects on the neglected side.

Line bisection. Patients are asked to mark the midpoint of a horizontal line. Patients with hemispatial neglect characteristically place the mark toward the side of the lesion, ignoring the contralateral part of the line.

Cancellation tasks. Patients are asked to cross out all examples of a particular target on a page. Patients with neglect omit targets on the side opposite the lesion.

Drawing and copying. When asked to copy a drawing or draw a clock, patients with neglect may omit details on the contralateral side or crowd all of the numbers on one side of the clock face.

Extinction. Extinction is the failure to perceive a stimulus on one side when both sides are stimulated simultaneously, despite the ability to perceive the stimulus when it is presented alone. Extinction can be tested in the visual, auditory, and tactile modalities by presenting bilateral simultaneous stimulation.
