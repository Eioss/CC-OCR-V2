Metallic Bonding

Metallic bonding is the electrostatic attraction between positive metal ions and the delocalized electrons surrounding them. In a metal, the valence electrons are not bound to any particular atom but are free to move throughout the structure. These free electrons form an electron cloud or electron sea around the positive metal ions.

The presence of these delocalized electrons explains many properties of metals, such as electrical conductivity, thermal conductivity, malleability, ductility, metallic lustre, and high melting and boiling points. Since metallic bonds are non-directional, layers of metal ions can slide over one another without breaking the bond, making metals malleable and ductile.
