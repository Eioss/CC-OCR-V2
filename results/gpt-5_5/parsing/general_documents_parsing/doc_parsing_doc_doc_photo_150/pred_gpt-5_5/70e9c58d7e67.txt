\begin{center}
\underline{The Discriminant}
\end{center}

The formula for finding the discriminant is derived \\
from the quadratic equation.

\[
\text{Discriminant formula: } b^2 - 4ac
\]

When:

\[
\ast\ b^2 - 4ac > 0
\]

\[
\ast\ \text{there are two unequal real solutions.}
\]

\[
\ast\ \text{if the discriminant is a perfect square,}
\]
\[
\text{the solutions are rational.}
\]

\[
\ast\ \text{if the discriminant is not a perfect square,}
\]
\[
\text{the solutions are irrational}
\]

\[
\ast\ b^2 - 4ac = 0
\]

\[
\ast\ \text{there is one solution that is a real number}
\]

\[
\ast\ b^2 - 4ac < 0
\]

\[
\ast\ \text{there are no real solutions}
\]

\[
\ast\ \text{there are two imaginary solutions}
\]

\[
\ast\ \text{the solutions are complex conjugates}
\]
