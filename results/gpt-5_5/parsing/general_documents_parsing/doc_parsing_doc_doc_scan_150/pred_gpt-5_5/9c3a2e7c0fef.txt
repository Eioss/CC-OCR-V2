\begin{center}
\textit{A proverbial Phrase.}
\end{center}

Dea dombvilda vöga dæ vilda vöga pêsa (or passion) vöga.  
Given with following explanation: \textit{Easter-Sunday weather will last
all the week.} More correct probably: \textit{the weather of Passion-week
will be the weather of Easter-week.}

The Cunningsburgh phrase (acc. to Low): ``Myrk in e Liora, Luce
in e Liunga, Tim in e Quest in e geungna'' (It's dark in the Chim-
ney, but it's light thro' the Heath, it's still time for the stranger to
be gone).

In Yell (in ``de Herra'') this phrase is still preserved in the fol-
lowing form:

Mørka lôra, liestra lunga, tâmra gøstra gøngara [myrkt er í fjóra,
ljóst er í lyngi, tími er, at gestr(inn) gengr]. With regard to the form
gøngara, cf. Fær. gongur.

\bigskip

Our Lord's Prayer\footnote{A man still living in Unst was formerly able, according to his own and other's
statements, to recite Our Lord's Prayer in Norn, or at any rate, the greater part of
it. Some time ago, however, he had an illness which gradually enfeebled his mem-
ory, and when I looked him up, he could only remember the following corrupted
fragments; --- \textit{halagä war'ta name} (hallowed be Thy name) --- \textit{sae
lembîns} (as in Heaven).} from Foula (reported by Low in his ``Tour
thro' Orkney and Shetland'', reprinted by Barry and in ``Annaler for
nordisk oldkyndighed'' 1860):

Fy vor o er i Chimeri. Halaght vara nam det. La Konungdum
din cumma. La vill din vera guerde i virildin sinda eri Chimeri. Gav
vus dagh u dagloght brau. Forgive sindor vasa (wara?) sin vi for-
givea gem ao sinda gainst wus. Lia wus eke o vera tempa, but de-
livra wus fro adlu idlu, for do i ir Konungdum, u puri, u glori. Amen.

\begin{center}
\textit{An Incantation.}
\end{center}

This is found in the tale: ``Da Tief i' de Neaan'' (Shetland
Times 1879).

The tale itself, as well as the language used in it, doubtless or-
iginates from Al. (Wests.).

The formula, which is mainly in Scoto-English, has Norn words
sprinkled throughout, some of which show old grammatical endings,
that have lost their significance. It runs as follows:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{l}
Da stuhl es scarp an fien, \\
Da sacheles emer a snean, \\
Da vird es sixer en puna, \\
A glimer i' mirk-as-dim hura, \\
La stuhl an vird ay gyrda \\
An prof er an skyla, an svirda.
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
