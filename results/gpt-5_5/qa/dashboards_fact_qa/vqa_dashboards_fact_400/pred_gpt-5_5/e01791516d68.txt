The main phases (states) of matter in chemistry are:

1. **Solid** — has a fixed shape and fixed volume. Particles are tightly packed and vibrate in place.  
   *Example: ice, salt, metal*

2. **Liquid** — has a fixed volume but takes the shape of its container. Particles are close together but can move past each other.  
   *Example: water, oil, mercury*

3. **Gas** — has no fixed shape or volume; it expands to fill its container. Particles are far apart and move freely.  
   *Example: oxygen, carbon dioxide, water vapor*

4. **Plasma** — an ionized gas made of charged particles. It occurs at very high temperatures or energies.  
   *Example: lightning, the Sun, neon signs*

In many basic chemistry courses, the three most commonly discussed phases are **solid, liquid, and gas**.
