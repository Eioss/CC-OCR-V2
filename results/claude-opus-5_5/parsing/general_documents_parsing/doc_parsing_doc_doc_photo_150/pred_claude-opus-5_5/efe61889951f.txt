\section*{Summary of the Abstract (paraphrased)}
The study measured soil hydro-physical properties, water-holding capacity, and
infiltration under typical forest types on the southern slopes of the Xiao Xing'an
Mountains. It then fitted three infiltration models (Kostiakov, Horton, Philip).

\begin{itemize}
  \item Bulk density ranged from about $0.45$ to $1.52~\mathrm{g/cm^3}$.
  \item Saturated, capillary, and natural moisture contents all declined with depth.
  \item Maximum water storage was roughly $283$--$341~\mathrm{mm}$.
  \item Effective storage was roughly $28$--$61~\mathrm{mm}$.
  \item Precipitation retention was roughly $166$--$197~\mathrm{mm}$. The exception was a
        planted \textit{Larix gmelinii} stand, which was much lower.
  \item Initial and steady infiltration rates peaked in the $0$--$20~\mathrm{cm}$ layers.
  \item The Horton model gave the best fit.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} soil hydrological properties; water storage capacity;
infiltration; Xiao Xing'an Mountains

\section*{Infiltration Models Referenced (standard forms)}
Kostiakov:
\begin{equation}
  f(t) = a\,t^{-b}
\end{equation}
Horton:
\begin{equation}
  f(t) = f_c + (f_0 - f_c)\,e^{-kt}
\end{equation}
Philip:
\begin{equation}
  f(t) = \tfrac{1}{2}\,S\,t^{-1/2} + A
\end{equation}
where $f$ is the infiltration rate, $f_0$ and $f_c$ are the initial and steady rates,
$S$ is sorptivity, and $a$, $b$, $k$, $A$ are fitted parameters.
