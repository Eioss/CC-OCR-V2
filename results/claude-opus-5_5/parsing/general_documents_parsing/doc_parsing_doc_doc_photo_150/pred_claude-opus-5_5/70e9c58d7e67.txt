\section*{The Discriminant}

The formula for finding the discriminant is derived from the quadratic equation.

\medskip

\noindent Discriminant formula: \quad $b^2 - 4ac$

\medskip

\noindent When:

\begin{itemize}
    \item[$\ast$] $b^2 - 4ac > 0$
    \begin{itemize}
        \item[$\ast$] there are two unequal real solutions.
        \item[$\ast$] if the discriminant is a perfect square, the solutions are rational.
        \item[$\ast$] if the discriminant is not a perfect square, the solutions are irrational
    \end{itemize}

    \item[$\ast$] $b^2 - 4ac = 0$
    \begin{itemize}
        \item[$\ast$] there is one solution that is a real number
    \end{itemize}

    \item[$\ast$] $b^2 - 4ac < 0$
    \begin{itemize}
        \item[$\ast$] there are no real solutions
        \item[$\ast$] there are two imaginary solutions
        \item[$\ast$] the solutions are complex conjugates
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
