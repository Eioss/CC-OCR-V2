Step 5 & $x^2 + \dfrac{b}{a}x + \dfrac{b^2}{4a^2} = -\dfrac{4ac}{4a^2} + \dfrac{b^2}{4a^2}$ \\

Step 6 & $\left(x + \dfrac{b}{2a}\right)^2 = \dfrac{b^2 - 4ac}{4a^2}$ \\
\hline
Step 7 & $\left(x + \dfrac{b}{2a}\right) = \pm\sqrt{\dfrac{b^2 - 4ac}{4a^2}} = \pm\dfrac{\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{4a}$ \\
\hline
Step 8 & $x = -\dfrac{b}{2a} \pm \dfrac{\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$ \\
\hline
Step 9 & $x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$ \\
\hline

**Notes:**
- Step 7 is transcribed exactly as shown, with $4a$ in the final denominator.
- The handwritten margin notes are only partly legible, so they appear as approximate LaTeX comments.

On the math itself, Step 7 is the first incorrect step, not the selected Step 4. Since $\sqrt{4a^2} = 2a$, the expression should be $\pm\frac{\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}$. Step 4 is correct because it adds $\left(\frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 = \frac{b^2}{4a^2}$ to both sides to complete the square.
