## Phases of Matter

The **"Phases"** section of the map (in the green "Rules of Chemistry" area) shows the three classic phases:

### 1. Solid 🧊
- Particles are tightly packed in a fixed arrangement and only vibrate in place.
- Solids have a **definite shape and volume**.
- Examples: ice, metals, salt crystals.

### 2. Liquid 💧
- Particles are close together but can slide past one another.
- Liquids have a **definite volume** but take the **shape of their container**.
- Examples: water, oil, mercury.

### 3. Gas 💨
- Particles are far apart and move rapidly and freely.
- Gases have **no fixed shape or volume** and expand to fill their container.
- Examples: oxygen, steam, carbon dioxide.

### How Phases Change
The map's arrows show that **pressure and temperature** control phase:
- **High pressure / low temperature** → pushes matter toward **solid**
- **Low pressure / high temperature** → pushes matter toward **gas**

Common transitions include melting (solid → liquid), freezing (liquid → solid), evaporation (liquid → gas), condensation (gas → liquid), sublimation (solid → gas), and deposition (gas → solid).

### Beyond the Basic Three
The map doesn't show these, but scientists also recognize other phases:
- **Plasma**: ionized gas found in stars, lightning, and neon signs (often called the 4th state of matter)
- **Bose-Einstein condensates**: formed near absolute zero
- **Supercritical fluids**: having properties of both liquid and gas at high temperature and pressure
- **Liquid crystals**: used in LCD screens
