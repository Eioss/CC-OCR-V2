Based on the provided map from Cabela’s “State of the Union 2015: The United States of Turkey,” we can determine which turkey type occupies the **largest contiguous geographic area** in the U.S.

Looking at the color-coded key and the map:

- **Eastern Wild Turkey (teal/blue)** — Covers a vast, continuous region across the eastern half of the U.S., including states like Maine, New York, Pennsylvania, West Virginia, Virginia, North Carolina, Tennessee, Kentucky, Ohio, Indiana, Illinois, Missouri, Arkansas, Louisiana, Mississippi, Alabama, Georgia, Florida (partially), and extending into parts of Michigan, Wisconsin, Minnesota, Iowa, Kansas, Oklahoma, and even portions of Texas and Nebraska. This is clearly the most widespread subspecies shown.

- **Merriam’s Wild Turkey (purple)** — Found primarily in the western mountainous regions (Rockies, Sierra Nevada, Cascades) and parts of the northern Plains (e.g., Montana, Wyoming, Colorado, Utah, Idaho). Significant but more fragmented and less extensive than Eastern.

- **Rio Grande Wild Turkey (red/orange)** — Concentrated in Texas, Oklahoma, New Mexico, Arizona, and parts of Louisiana and Arkansas. Large area, but not as broad as Eastern.

- **Osceola Wild Turkey (yellow/orange)** — Limited to central Florida only. Very small range.

- **Hybrid Turkey (green)** — Scattered patches mainly in the Midwest and South-Central U.S. (e.g., Kansas, Oklahoma, Missouri, Arkansas). Not dominant in any single large region.

✅ **Conclusion**:  
The **Eastern Wild Turkey** is found in the **majority of the country by landmass**, covering the entire eastern seaboard, Appalachia, the Southeast, and much of the Midwest — making it the most geographically widespread subspecies depicted on this map.
